\title{Strict-EM Inverts Cross-Lingual VLM Rankings:\\An Architecture-Specific Measurement Artifact}

\author{Anonymous Submission}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Multilingual document VLMs are ranked on benchmarks scored by strict exact-match (EM) against a single gold string. On three open VLMs over the full MTVQA validation set ($n=2142$ jointly judged rows) and a 1394-row XFUND replicate, we show strict-EM systematically penalises semantically-correct answers in an \emph{architecture-specific} way that reverses cross-VLM rankings on identical inputs. Under a vision-grounded Gemini-2.5-Flash semantic-EM judge (cross-validated against Gemini-2.5-Pro at $89\%$ 3-way agreement, $\kappa{\approx}0.82$, and against a 50-row blind human annotator at $\kappa{=}0.61$), InternVL-2.5-8B leads Qwen2.5-VL-7B by $+5.5$ pp on strict-EM while Qwen leads by $+11.95$ pp on semantic-EM-C; the inversion holds in $100\%$ of 2000 paired bootstrap resamples. Decomposed by architecture, the Latin/non-Latin ``artifact share''---the fraction of the script gap explained by surface-form mismatch rather than capability---spans 121 pp across three VLMs (Qwen $+66\%$, InternVL $+8\%$, Phi-3.5-vision $-57\%$); the Qwen-vs-Phi difference is bootstrap-significant. Across 13 plausible cross-lingual interventions on Qwen-7B, aggregate $\Delta$strict moves up to $+4.2$ pp while aggregate $\Delta$semantic-EM-C is statistically null ($-0.28$ pp [$-4.98$, $+4.42$] for the largest-lifting SFT recipe), and per-language point estimates show direction-of-effect anti-correlation on non-Latin scripts. We propose a nine-item reporting protocol (R1--R9) deployable at single-digit dollar cost: report both metrics per language with CIs, validate the judge against a cross-family second judge or human annotators, and release the (image, gold, prediction, verdict, reason) tuples for third-party verification. The implication is that a substantial part of recently reported cross-lingual strict-EM lift literature is, on re-measurement against the image, surface-form normalisation; and that the choice of multilingual VLM from a strict-EM leaderboard is the choice of architecture whose output-style prior matches gold convention, not the model that more often produces a semantically correct image-grounded answer.
\end{abstract}
